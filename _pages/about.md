I am Junyu Guo (James), a Ph.D. candidate at the University of California, Berkeley, where I am fortunate to be advised by [Prof. Javad Lavaei](https://lavaei.ieor.berkeley.edu/index.html). My research asks how agents should make decisions when the evidence is incomplete, and how we can trust the decisions they make.

Three threads run through my recent work. In reinforcement learning, [DRCORL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12391) (NeurIPS 2025) shows that offline policies can satisfy safety constraints without trading off reward or inference speed. In LLM reasoning, I study how models should [express their uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05306), [choose a reasoning style](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20868) that fits the problem, and [keep track of information that changes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38866) within a conversation. Most recently, [Groundability, Not Scale Alone](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01023) asks when a small model can reliably audit the patches of a much stronger coding agent, a question that grows more urgent as agents take over more of software development.

I am also fortunate to work with [Prof. Costas Spanos](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Faculty/Homepages/spanos.html) (Berkeley EECS), [Prof. Ming Jin](https://ece.vt.edu/people/profile/jinming.html) (Virginia Tech) and [Dr. Shangding Gu](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~shangding.gu/index.html), as well as Liyuan Liang and [Yuchen Fang](https://ychenfang.github.io/) from Prof. Lavaei's group.

Prior to Berkeley, I completed my undergraduate studies in the [Department of Mathematical Sciences at Tsinghua University](https://www.math.tsinghua.edu.cn/) in Beijing, China, where I was advised by [Prof. Chenxu Li](https://en.gsm.pku.edu.cn/faculty/cxli/) and [Prof. Yiwen Shen](https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/faculty-and-staff/directory/yiwenshen) on efficient simulation for portfolio allocation in incomplete markets. During my time at Tsinghua, I was also an exchange student at Cornell University, collaborating with [Prof. Andreea Minca](https://www.engineering.cornell.edu/faculty-directory/andreea-c-minca) and [Prof. Qiaomin Xie](https://qiaominxie.github.io/) on reinforcement learning for mean field games.
