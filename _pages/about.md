I'm Junyu Guo, and most people call me James. I'm a Ph.D. candidate at UC Berkeley, advised by [Prof. Javad Lavaei](https://lavaei.ieor.berkeley.edu/index.html), and my work circles one question from two sides: how should an AI agent decide when the evidence is incomplete, and how can we trust the decision it makes?

Three threads run through my recent work. In reinforcement learning, [DRCORL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12391) (NeurIPS 2025) shows that an offline policy can honor safety constraints without trading away reward or inference speed. In language models, I study how a model should [express its uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05306), [choose a reasoning style](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20868) that fits the problem, and [keep track of information that changes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38866) mid-conversation. Most recently, [Groundability, Not Scale Alone](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01023) asks when a small model can reliably audit the patches of a much stronger coding agent, a question that grows more pressing as agents take on more of software development.

I'm fortunate to collaborate with [Prof. Costas Spanos](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Faculty/Homepages/spanos.html) (Berkeley EECS), [Prof. Ming Jin](https://ece.vt.edu/people/profile/jinming.html) (Virginia Tech) and [Dr. Shangding Gu](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~shangding.gu/index.html), and with Liyuan Liang and [Yuchen Fang](https://ychenfang.github.io/) from our group.

Before Berkeley, I studied mathematics at [Tsinghua University](https://www.math.tsinghua.edu.cn/), where [Prof. Chenxu Li](https://en.gsm.pku.edu.cn/faculty/cxli/) and [Prof. Yiwen Shen](https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/faculty-and-staff/directory/yiwenshen) introduced me to research through efficient simulation for portfolio allocation in incomplete markets. An exchange semester at Cornell with [Prof. Andreea Minca](https://www.engineering.cornell.edu/faculty-directory/andreea-c-minca) and [Prof. Qiaomin Xie](https://qiaominxie.github.io/) brought me to reinforcement learning, by way of mean field games.
