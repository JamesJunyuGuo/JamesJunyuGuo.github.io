I'm Junyu Guo, James to most people. I'm a Ph.D. candidate at UC Berkeley, advised by [Prof. Javad Lavaei](https://lavaei.ieor.berkeley.edu/index.html). I design efficient decision-making algorithms for agents that learn from data and act under uncertainty, from safe reinforcement learning to the way large language models reason, and I care about making the resulting systems ones we can actually trust.

My work so far runs along three threads. In reinforcement learning, [DRCORL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12391) (NeurIPS 2025) shows that an offline policy can keep its safety promises without paying for them in reward or inference speed; the title is the thesis, *don't trade off safety*. In language models, I'm drawn to the habits of a careful thinker: knowing when to [say "I'm not sure"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05306), [picking a reasoning style](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20868) that fits the problem rather than defaulting to one, and [noticing when a fact has changed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38866) halfway through a conversation. And in trustworthy AI, my most recent paper, [Groundability, Not Scale Alone](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01023), asks when a small model can reliably audit the work of a much stronger coding agent. The answer has less to do with how big the reviewer is than with whether its evidence can actually be checked.

None of this is solo work. I'm fortunate to collaborate with [Prof. Costas Spanos](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Faculty/Homepages/spanos.html) (Berkeley EECS), [Prof. Ming Jin](https://ece.vt.edu/people/profile/jinming.html) (Virginia Tech) and [Dr. Shangding Gu](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~shangding.gu/index.html), and with Liyuan Liang and [Yuchen Fang](https://ychenfang.github.io/) from our group.

I came to all of this by way of mathematics. At [Tsinghua University](https://www.math.tsinghua.edu.cn/), [Prof. Chenxu Li](https://en.gsm.pku.edu.cn/faculty/cxli/) and [Prof. Yiwen Shen](https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/faculty-and-staff/directory/yiwenshen) showed me what research feels like, through the problem of allocating a portfolio efficiently when markets are incomplete. An exchange semester at Cornell with [Prof. Andreea Minca](https://www.engineering.cornell.edu/faculty-directory/andreea-c-minca) and [Prof. Qiaomin Xie](https://qiaominxie.github.io/), working on mean field games, was my first taste of reinforcement learning, and I never quite left.
